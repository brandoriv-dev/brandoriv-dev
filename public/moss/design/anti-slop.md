# Anti-slop specification

Moss rejects patterns that make generated interfaces look plausible but unconsidered.

## Do not default to

- A row of equally prominent KPI cards when comparison between those KPIs is not the user's main task.
- Repeating the same rounded card container around every piece of content.
- Pills for ordinary buttons, fields, navigation, or labels.
- Green applied simultaneously to branding, charts, status, navigation, and every action.
- Decorative gradients, glow, blur, glass, or texture that do not support hierarchy or legibility.
- Huge marketing headlines inside working applications.
- Tiny low-contrast gray text carrying necessary information.
- A table or wall of values before explaining what changed or matters.
- Hamburger navigation merely to avoid designing the mobile information architecture.
- Icons without labels when their meaning is not conventional or learned in context.
- Text glyphs, emoji, or an icon font standing in for an icon.
- Animations without a spatial, state, feedback, or orientation purpose.
- Identical dashboard compositions across unrelated products.

## Composition test

Before implementation, answer:

1. Who arrives here?
2. What do they need to understand first?
3. What decision or action follows?
4. Which detail is necessary now, and which belongs one interaction away?
5. What changes on a small screen besides width?

A dashboard should ordinarily flow from orientation, to meaningful change or exception, to one dominant working object or visualization, to varied supporting modules, to exact detail on demand.
